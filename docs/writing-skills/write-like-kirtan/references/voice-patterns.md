# Voice patterns

Use these patterns as a guide, not a template. Give the user's current wording and corrections priority.

## Core rhythm

- Lead with the need or action: "give me," "make," "use," "I want," "what we need is." Use these openings only for requests; keep a story, update, or opinion in its original communicative form.
- Put the practical result before explanation. Add a reason with "so" or "because" and a constraint with "but," "if," or "and also."
- Allow additive, spoken-feeling structure: main point, another detail, then the important qualifier. Avoid forcing everything into perfectly parallel bullet points.
- Use plain, concrete words. Favor "shorter," "real," "human," "how it works," and "what we need" over abstract or promotional phrasing when they express the same meaning.
- Use short follow-ups and immediate corrections when the source is short. Retain the full chain of details when the source is detailed; brevity must not remove constraints.
- Preserve modest repetition when it conveys emphasis or clarifies scope. Phrases like "as well," "like that," and "and also" can be natural. Avoid repetitive catchphrases.
- Use lowercase openings often in casual messages, but retain mixed casing. Capitalized sentences and "I" are also natural. Keep proper nouns and identifiers intact.
- Retain ordinary apostrophes: "don't" and "that's" are natural. Expanded "I am" and "I will" are also natural; do not automatically contract every phrase.
- Keep punctuation light in chat. No final period can be natural; a real question can retain its question mark. Do not insert "??", "dude," irritation, or urgency into neutral text.
- Prefer simple prose over styled headings and elaborate structure, unless the requested format needs those elements.

## Light irregularity

Retain some compressed articles/prepositions and naturally uneven sentence construction when the meaning stays clear. For example, a request may sound like "one guide for how this works" rather than "a comprehensive guide explaining the operation of the system."

Treat the following spellings as individually observed possibilities, not proven recurring habits or a replacement dictionary:

- unnecessary → unneccesary
- particular → perticuler
- grammar → grammer
- concise → consice
- transcription → transcrption

Allow a form only when the original word already belongs in the rewrite, the user wants the close-natural or raw voice, and no ambiguity or precision risk results. Usually prefer the less disruptive forms. Do not alter titles, technical terms, labels, or identifiers even if they contain those words. A short rewrite may contain no misspelling at all.

The closed-up forms "coverletter" and "coverletters" recur, while "cover letter" is also natural. Allow either in informal requests about that document, without joining unrelated compounds.

Do not imitate missing negation letters, accidental pasted strings, explicit corrections, or random missing spaces. Do not recreate a particular typo simply to prove that the skill is active. No error rate or fixed quota is established.

## Synthetic examples

Use these examples for cadence only. They are illustrative rewrites, not personal facts or historical quotes. Do not reuse their content in other drafts.

### Direct request

Source: "Please create one short guide explaining how the app works. Include the main steps and keep the explanation simple."

Close-natural: "give me one short guide for how the app works and add the main steps as well, keep it simple"

### Incremental constraints

Source: "Make the introduction shorter. Also keep the example, but do not add a new section."

Close-natural: "make intro shorter and also keep the example, but don't add a new section"

### Statement, not a command

Source: "I checked the draft, and most of it works well. The ending still needs a little more explanation."

Close-natural: "I checked the draft and most of it works well, the ending still needs a bit more explanation"

### Precise conditional request

Source: "If v2.4.1 fails, use v2.3.8. Do not change API_TIMEOUT=30."

Close-natural: "if v2.4.1 fails use v2.3.8, but don't change API_TIMEOUT=30"

### Raw with one harmless irregularity

Source: "Remove the unnecessary introduction and keep the three steps."

Raw: "remove the unneccesary intro and keep the three steps"

Clean: "Remove the unnecessary intro and keep the three steps."

### Work message

Source: "Hi Sam, I have reviewed the proposal. I am not ready to approve it because the estimate is missing. Could you add the estimate and send it again?"

Close-natural: "Hi Sam, I checked the proposal but I am not ready to approve it because the estimate is missing. Can you add the estimate and send it again?"

### Personal prose without an invented story

Source: "I enjoy building simple tools that people actually use. I would like this role because it includes direct feedback from users."

Close-natural: "I like building simple tools that people actually use, and I would like this role because it includes feedback directly from users"

Keep "enjoy" if changing it to "like" would weaken an important emphasis. Keep the original degree of interest or commitment whenever it matters.
